Business handbook

KPI Encyclopedia

This handbook is the primary reference when an owner sees a KPI on a BTR Portal dashboard and needs to know what it means, why it matters, how to read it, when to worry, what to check next, and what to do. It is not a technical, database, or formula manual.

Business intent
Help owners, directors, and general managers use dashboard KPIs for decision making across Customer, Salesman, Item, and Supplier.
Data source
BTR Portal management dashboards (read-only analytics from BTR operational data). Desktop remains the place where transactions are entered and operational actions are completed.

How to use this handbook

Open the dashboard, find the KPI name, then find the same name here. Read What it means and How to read first. If the number looks wrong for the business, follow Next KPI to check before changing credit, routes, or purchasing.

KPI names match the portal as closely as possible. Indonesian business words used in BTR — Piutang, Omzet, Faktur, Plafond, Principal, Wilayah — are kept because that is how the business speaks.

Where a dashboard does not publish a numeric trigger, this handbook does not invent one. Direction (high/low) is a general reading, not a target. Forecast views look 30 days ahead using business rules; they are not automatic credit holds, purchase orders, or write-offs.

  2. Salesman — targets, field productivity, book quality, rankings, capacity.
  3. Item — inventory value, aging risk, forecast, replenishment actions.
  4. Supplier — principal dependence, supplier risk, purchase contribution, posting quality.

1. Customer

Purpose

This area exists so management can see customers as both a source of omzet and a store of company cash (Piutang). It combines what is happening now, what may happen in the next 30 days, and what Finance and Sales should do today with each account.

Why the owner should care

A distribution business lives on repeat customers paying on time. When the book weakens, three losses arrive together: slower cash, quieter offtake, and credit given without approval. Totals on a finance report cannot show which relationships to grow, protect, collect, recover, or exit.

Typical decisions

  • Whether to continue, restrict, or stop supply to a customer.
  • Where collection time should go today versus this week.
  • Which relationships are strategic enough for owner involvement.
  • Whether revenue growth is coming from a healthy book or from a shrinking, more concentrated book.
  • When dormant and declining customers should be recovered versus released.

Owner lens. Understand the health, risk, contribution, credit discipline, and collection actions of the customer book.

Business questions this area answers

  • How healthy is the customer book over the next 30 days?
  • How much receivable is outstanding, and how much of it is already late?
  • Which customers create the highest cash and relationship risk?
  • Is the book still buying, going quiet, or never converted?
  • Are we collecting as fast as we are billing?
  • Who should be contacted today, and is that work collection, credit, or sales recovery?
  • Is credit policy actually being respected?

1.1 Kesehatan Portofolio Customer

1.1.1 Customer Risk Assessment

1.1.1.1

Portfolio Health Score

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

A single score of how healthy the customer book looks over the next 30 days. It combines how much receivable sits with customers already showing elevated risk, and how many active customers sit in High Risk or Critical.

This is a forward-looking management score, not a credit decision. It does not place a hold, suspend an account, or change plafond.

Why it matters

Owners use it as the headline for preventive collection and credit planning. A weakening score means problems are forming before they become overdue or dormant.

How to read

HighA higher score generally means a healthier book: fewer customers in serious forward-risk categories and less receivable sitting with them.
LowA lower score generally means risk is spreading across the book. Collection and credit attention should move earlier, not wait for aging to worsen.

Warning signs

  • Score moving down from one review to the next.
  • Score looking healthy while Customers At Risk Count is rising — the average may hide a growing tail.
  • Score used as if it were an automatic credit stop. It is indicative only.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Portfolio Healthy %Customers At Risk CountHigh Risk Customer CountElevated Risk Receivable %

Next KPI to check

  1. Check Portfolio Healthy % — is the healthy share shrinking?
  2. Check Customers At Risk Count and High Risk Customer Count.
  3. Open Collection Action Queue if the score is falling and overdue is already material.

Typical owner action

  • Ask Sales and Finance to review the Customer Risk Forecast together.
  • Do not change credit policy from this score alone — confirm with risk categories and overdue exposure.

Common misinterpretations

Do not treat this score as profitability, as a credit-limit calculation, or as an automatic hold. Customer value in the portfolio is invoiced omzet, not margin.

1.1.1.2

Portfolio Healthy %

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

The share of customers whose 30-day forecast category is Healthy. It answers: of everyone in the book, how many currently show no forward-risk signals?

Why it matters

It is the executive headline for portfolio quality. It complements older, backward-looking signals such as overdue and dormant, which only appear after the problem has already happened.

How to read

HighA high percentage means most customers have no forecast risk signals. The book is broadly clean.
LowA low percentage means a large share of customers already carry Watch or worse. Management time will be consumed by protection and collection rather than growth.

Warning signs

  • Healthy share shrinking while Active Customer Count is stable — quality is deteriorating inside the same book.
  • Healthy % looking strong while Strategic Customers At Risk Count is rising — the important accounts may be the ones weakening.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Portfolio Health ScoreRisk Category - HealthyCustomers At Risk CountStrategic Customers At Risk Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Check Risk Category - Watch and Attention — those are the next customers to leave Healthy.
  2. Check Strategic Customers At Risk Count.
  3. Check Customers Forecasted At Risk for the full at-risk population.

Typical owner action

  • Review Customer Portfolio with Sales leadership.
  • Protect strategic accounts first if they are leaving the Healthy category.

Common misinterpretations

No common interpretation issue identified.

1.1.1.3

Customers At Risk Count

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers sit in Watch, Attention, High Risk, or Critical on the 30-day forecast. This is the breadth of forward risk — how many accounts need preventive attention.

Why it matters

It is an early-warning count. Management uses it to size collection, credit, and sales-recovery workload before overdue or dormancy appears.

How to read

HighMore customers are showing forward-risk signals. Preventive work is spreading across a wider book.
LowFewer customers need early intervention. Attention can stay concentrated on the remaining cases.

Warning signs

  • Count rising mid-month while overdue still looks calm — risk is forming ahead of aging.
  • Count high but Collection Action Queue is light — the forecast is not being converted into daily work.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Customers Forecasted At RiskHigh Risk Customer CountRisk Category - WatchRisk Category - AttentionPortfolio Health Score

Next KPI to check

  1. Split the count using the five Risk Category KPIs.
  2. Check High Risk Customer Count and Risk Category - Critical for severity.
  3. Move to Collection Action Queue for who to contact today.

Typical owner action

  • Allocate Finance and Sales time to the at-risk list.
  • Treat this as a planning number, not as an automatic credit freeze.

Common misinterpretations

This count includes Watch. It is wider than High Risk Customer Count. Do not treat every at-risk customer as already overdue.

1.1.1.4

Strategic Customers At Risk Count

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many Strategic-tier customers sit in Watch or worse. These are the highest-importance accounts — material omzet and/or receivable — that are also showing forward risk.

Why it matters

A strategic account in trouble is not the same as a small account in trouble. This KPI tells the owner whether the customers that matter most to the business are the ones weakening.

How to read

HighImportant customers are carrying forward risk. Revenue, relationship, and working capital are all exposed together.
LowStrategic accounts are largely clean. Remaining risk is more likely in smaller or less critical customers.

Warning signs

  • Any rise in this count — even a few accounts — because the commercial impact is concentrated.
  • Strategic count rising while overall Customers At Risk looks stable — risk is migrating into the most important names.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Strategic Customer CountCustomers At Risk CountTop Omzet Customer %Working Capital Tied AmountManagement Escalation Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Identify which strategic customers are at risk in Customer Portfolio.
  2. Check their overdue, plafond, and purchase-decline signals.
  3. If collection is already late, check Management Escalation Count.

Typical owner action

  • Owner or GM should review the named strategic accounts directly with the sales manager.
  • Do not apply a generic credit squeeze to the whole book until these accounts are understood.

Common misinterpretations

Strategic is a computed portfolio tier. It is not the same as Klasifikasi on customer master data.

1.1.1.5

High Risk Customer Count

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers sit in High Risk or Critical — the severe end of the 30-day forecast. These accounts already combine multiple strong warning signals.

Why it matters

This is the severity cut of the at-risk population. It tells management how many customers need intervention now, not merely monitoring.

How to read

HighMany customers have stacked payment, credit, inactivity, decline, or collection signals. Escalation and credit review will be heavy.
LowSevere cases are few. Most at-risk customers are still in Watch or Attention.

Warning signs

  • Count rising while Overdue Customer Count is still modest — the forecast is ahead of aging.
  • Count concentrated among strategic or top-omzet customers.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Risk Category - High RiskRisk Category - CriticalCustomers At Risk CountElevated Risk ReceivableManagement Escalation Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Split High Risk vs Critical using the category counts.
  2. Check Elevated Risk Receivable to see how much money sits with these customers.
  3. Check Collection Action Queue — Immediate Collection and Management Escalation.

Typical owner action

  • Prioritize named high-risk accounts with Finance and Sales the same day.
  • Review credit exposure before releasing further shipments to these customers.

Common misinterpretations

High Risk Customer Count is narrower than Customers At Risk Count. Watch and Attention are excluded.

1.1.1.6

Customers Forecasted At Risk

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

The Customer Risk Forecast view of customers expected to become a problem within 30 days — overdue, over limit, inactive, or needing early collection. Same population idea as Customers At Risk Count, shown on the forecast dashboard.

Why it matters

Use it when asking “who will become a problem?” rather than “who is already overdue?”. It is the planning list for preventive work.

How to read

HighA large forward-risk population. Collection planning and sales recovery should start before month-end aging deteriorates.
LowFew customers are forecasted into risk. Monitor Watch-category movement.

Warning signs

  • Forecasted at-risk rising while current overdue is flat.
  • Repeated appearance of the same customers across reviews.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Customers At Risk CountHigh Risk Customer CountPayment Delay Signal CountInactivity Signal CountPurchase Decline Signal Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Read the signal-family counts to see why they are forecasted at risk.
  2. Then open Collection Action Queue for today’s contact list.

Typical owner action

  • Run a mid-month risk review with Sales and Finance.
  • Convert the forecast list into named owners and next actions — the dashboard does not act by itself.

Common misinterpretations

This is an indicative forecast from business rules, not a prediction of default and not an automatic credit hold.

1.1.1.7

Risk Category - Healthy

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers have no material 30-day forecast signals — or have no balance and are still purchasing. This is the clean part of the book.

Why it matters

It shows how much of the portfolio can be managed for growth rather than protection. Together with the other four categories it is the risk distribution.

How to read

HighMost customers need only normal monitoring.
LowThe clean core of the book is shrinking. Growth capacity is being replaced by exception work.

Warning signs

  • Healthy count falling while total customer count is stable.
  • Healthy looking large while Strategic Customers At Risk is rising.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Portfolio Healthy %Risk Category - WatchActive Customer Count

Next KPI to check

  1. If Healthy is shrinking, check Watch next — that is the usual first step out of Healthy.
  2. Then Attention, High Risk, and Critical.

Typical owner action

  • Keep commercial focus on Healthy and Watch strategic accounts; do not let collection firefighting consume the whole book.

Common misinterpretations

No common interpretation issue identified.

1.1.1.8

Risk Category - Watch

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers have early, moderate forward-risk signals but are not yet in Attention. Typical entry is a single moderate signal or two weak signals, with no strong signal.

Why it matters

Watch is the cheapest place to intervene. These customers can often be protected with a reminder, a visit, or a credit check before they become overdue or dormant.

How to read

HighA wide early-warning belt. The team should work this list proactively.
LowFew early warnings. Either the book is clean, or customers are skipping Watch and landing in worse categories — which is itself a concern.

Warning signs

  • Watch shrinking while Attention and High Risk are growing — customers are deteriorating, not recovering.
  • Watch growing rapidly mid-month.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Proactive Reminder CountRisk Category - AttentionCustomers At Risk Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Check Proactive Reminder Count — are Watch customers being contacted before due date?
  2. If Watch is rolling into Attention, check Payment Delay and Credit Limit signals.

Typical owner action

  • Ask collection to work reminders on Watch accounts with a balance due soon.
  • Ask sales to visit Watch accounts that also show inactivity or purchase decline.

Common misinterpretations

No common interpretation issue identified.

1.1.1.9

Risk Category - Attention

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers have a strong signal, or several moderate signals, but are not yet High Risk. These accounts need a named owner and a specific next step.

Why it matters

Attention is the operating layer between monitoring and crisis. Leaving these customers unowned is how Watch becomes High Risk.

How to read

HighA large intervention queue. Sales and Finance must split the work or both will be late.
LowFew mid-severity cases. Confirm they are not hiding inside High Risk instead.

Warning signs

  • Attention count rising together with Due Within 7 Days.
  • Same customers remaining in Attention across reviews without an action.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Credit Review CountSales Recovery CountDue Within 7 DaysRisk Category - High Risk

Next KPI to check

  1. Split Attention customers by signal family: payment delay vs credit vs inactivity vs decline.
  2. Route collection cases to the action queue and sales-decline cases to Sales Recovery.

Typical owner action

  • Assign each Attention customer to Finance or Sales — not both by default, and not neither.
  • Review credit before expanding supply to Attention accounts with plafond pressure.

Common misinterpretations

No common interpretation issue identified.

1.1.1.10

Risk Category - High Risk

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers already combine several strong signals, or mix chronic problems with a new forward signal. These accounts are likely to become overdue, over-limit, or inactive if left alone.

Why it matters

This is the severity list for management intervention. It is the category that should drive credit review and collection priority.

How to read

HighMany accounts need firm collection and credit control now.
LowSevere cases are contained. Keep them from becoming Critical.

Warning signs

  • High Risk count rising among top omzet or top piutang customers.
  • High Risk customers still receiving new sales without a credit review.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

High Risk Customer CountRisk Category - CriticalCredit Review CountImmediate Collection CountElevated Risk Receivable

Next KPI to check

  1. List the named High Risk customers and their open balance.
  2. Check Immediate Collection Count and Credit Review Count.
  3. Check Strategic Customers At Risk Count.

Typical owner action

  • Stop treating these as routine follow-up. Review credit, collection plan, and sales continuation together.
  • Escalate strategic High Risk accounts to GM/owner.

Common misinterpretations

No common interpretation issue identified.

1.1.1.11

Risk Category - Critical

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers sit at the top of the forecast model — several strong signals, or a combination of chronic overdue, plafond pressure, and decline or inactivity. These are the most fragile relationships in the book.

Why it matters

Critical is a management-escalation population. Leaving these accounts to routine collection usually means more aging and more working capital stuck.

How to read

HighThe book has a cluster of accounts that can damage both cash and revenue at once.
LowFew extreme cases. Still review each one individually because impact is rarely small.

Warning signs

  • Any Critical strategic customer.
  • Critical count rising while Immediate Collection and Management Escalation queues are empty.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Management Escalation CountHigh Risk Customer CountPiutang > 90 HariSuspended + Sales Count

Next KPI to check

  1. Open Management Escalation Count and the named customer list.
  2. Check Piutang > 90 Hari and Plafond Breach Count for those customers.
  3. Check whether they are still being invoiced.

Typical owner action

  • Owner/GM should personally review Critical accounts with Sales and Finance.
  • Decide explicitly: collect and continue, collect and restrict, or exit review. Do not leave the decision implicit.

Common misinterpretations

Critical is a forecast category, not a legal write-off status. It does not by itself mean the debt is uncollectible.

1.1.2 Customer Lifecycle

1.1.2.1

Active Customer Count

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

How many customers were invoiced this month. Active means current billing activity, not that the customer is Healthy on the risk forecast.

Why it matters

It is the simplest measure of a living book. If active count falls, revenue breadth is shrinking even if a few large customers still produce omzet.

How to read

HighMore customers are buying this month. Market reach is broad.
LowFewer customers are billing. The company may be depending on a smaller core.

Warning signs

  • Active count falling while Total MTD Omzet is stable — concentration is increasing.
  • Active count looking healthy while Dormant Count is also rising — the book is rotating, not growing.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

2. Salesman

Purpose

This area exists because company results are produced by people walking routes, taking orders, and owning customer books. Outcome (omzet, achievement, piutang) and execution (visits, GPS, orders) answer different questions and must be read together.

Why the owner should care

A few high-omzet salesmen can hide a team that misses plans, skips routes, and leaves dormant customers on the book. When that happens, next month’s omzet and next quarter’s piutang both deteriorate. Field activity without orders is theatre; orders without visits on the plan is unmanaged coverage.

Typical decisions

  • Who needs coaching this week, and for what — plan, route, conversion, or collection.
  • Whether targets exist so performance can be judged.
  • Whether the company depends too much on one salesman for omzet or piutang.
  • Whether today’s field work covered the promised customers.
  • Which books carry overdue that Sales must own with Finance.

Owner lens. Judge the sales force on plan attainment, field execution, customer-book quality, and capacity — not on omzet alone.

Business questions this area answers

  • How many salesmen are behind a real target, and how many have no target at all?
  • Who is producing omzet, and is that production concentrated?
  • Did the team walk the planned route?
  • Did visits produce orders?
  • Are check-ins physically credible?
  • Which reps carry dormant customers or heavy overdue books?
  • Is the active force large enough for the plan?

3. Item

Purpose

This area exists because inventory is cash in physical form. Composition tells where the money sits. Risk tells what is not selling. Forecast tells what will run out. Optimization tells what to do today. Those four questions must not be collapsed into one total.

Why the owner should care

A high inventory value can fund sales or hide dead stock. Buying more because the warehouse “feels empty” on a few holes, while slow movers age, is how distributors trap cash. Owners need to fill holes, delay excess, and recover idle capital as separate decisions.

Typical decisions

  • Whether to release purchase cash this week.
  • Which categories and SKUs to clear versus replenish.
  • Whether unposted purchases should be posted before new buys.
  • When overstock and understock exist at the same time.
  • Which idle SKUs deserve a recovery programme.

Owner lens. See how much capital sits in stock, whether that stock is moving, whether active SKUs will run out, and what to buy, delay, transfer, or clear.

Business questions this area answers

  • How much capital is in the warehouse, and in which categories?
  • How much of that capital is slow, dead, or never sold?
  • Will active SKUs run out within 30 days?
  • Is the company overstocked, understocked, or both?
  • What is the first action today — buy, delay, transfer, post, or clear?
  • How much idle capital could still be recovered?

4. Supplier

Purpose

This area exists because BTR buys from principals and holds their goods. Dependence is not only “we buy a lot from them”. It is also “we already hold a lot”, “what we hold is not moving”, and “what we bought is not yet posted”.

Why the owner should care

A distributor that depends on one principal for spend and for warehouse capital can lose range, price power, and cash at the same time. Unposted invoices mean the company has already spent (or committed) without gaining sellable stock. Inactivity mid-month can be a freeze or a stall — those are different decisions.

Typical decisions

  • Whether dependence on a principal is chosen strategy or accidental risk.
  • Whether to keep buying a principal whose goods are already at risk in the warehouse.
  • Whether to post existing invoices before placing new ones.
  • Whether a quiet purchasing month is intentional.
  • Which principal relationships need owner-level attention.

Owner lens. See dependence on principals, the health of their stock, the scale of buying, and whether purchased goods actually become inventory.

Business questions this area answers

  • Who takes most of this month’s purchase cash?
  • Who already holds most of the warehouse capital?
  • Which principals dominate buying and stock (or sick stock) together?
  • Are purchased goods posted into inventory?
  • Is there an aged posting backlog?
  • Has purchasing gone quiet too late in the month?

The three-calendar-day aging is the approved qualified-backlog rule. Newer unposted invoices are pending, but not yet this KPI.

4.4.3 Purchasing Activity

4.4.3.1

Purchasing Inactivity Flag

Tap to open the owner briefing

What it means

A signal that no purchase invoices have been recorded this month after a mid-month point. It is a replenishment-gap flag, not a score.

Why it matters

If the warehouse is already long, inactivity can be healthy. If Stock-Out Risk Items are rising, inactivity is a service failure in progress.

How to read

HighThe flag is on — intake has gone quiet too late in the month to be “still planning”.
LowThe flag is off — purchasing documents exist, or it is still early.

Warning signs

  • Flag on plus stock-out risk high.
  • Flag on while Recommended Purchase Budget is also high — the plan exists, the documents do not.

No extra numeric trigger is stated here unless the source dashboards already use one.

Related KPIs

Grand Total PurchaseStock-Out Risk ItemsQualified Backlog Count & Value

Next KPI to check

  1. Confirm whether inactivity is a freeze (intentional) or a stall (process failure).
  2. Check stock-out list.

Typical owner action

  • If unintentional, restart purchasing immediately on critical SKUs.
  • If intentional, document that it is a freeze so the flag is not a surprise.

Common misinterpretations

This is a yes/no inactivity flag after a mid-month threshold, not a count of idle days. Exact clock time of “mid-month” beyond that description is a system setting — do not invent a calendar date here.

Executive summary — Supplier

Most important KPIs

  • Top 1 Principal % and Top Supplier % — spend versus stock dependence.
  • Compound Dependency Count.
  • Grand Total Purchase.
  • Posted % and Qualified Backlog Count & Value.
  • Principal At Risk Count.

Early warning KPIs

  • Purchasing Inactivity Flag after mid-month.
  • Pending Posting Value before it becomes qualified backlog.
  • Supplier Risk Exposure rising on a high-spend principal.

Investigation KPIs

  • Principal Exposure Comparison — names across spend, stock, and at-risk.
  • Top 10 Principal Ranking versus Top 10 Supplier Ranking.
  • Qualified Backlog on the same names as stock-out SKUs.

Recommended reading order

  1. Grand Total Purchase and Total Invoice.
  2. Top 1 / Top 3 Principal % and Top Principal %.
  3. Top Supplier % and Top 10 Supplier Ranking.
  4. Compound Dependency and Principal Exposure Comparison.
  5. Principal At Risk Count and Supplier Risk Exposure.
  6. Posted %, Pending Posting, Qualified Backlog.
  7. Purchasing Inactivity Flag.

Executive summary — all four areas

Read Customer for cash and relationship risk, Salesman for whether people are producing and covering, Item for whether warehouse capital is working, and Supplier for whether buying dependence and posting are under control. Problems that look like “sales is down” often start as missed visits, declining customers, or stock-outs on the SKUs that actually sell.

Most important KPIs

  • Portfolio Health Score, Total Piutang, Overdue Exposure, Recovery vs Billing %.
  • Below Target Count, Visit Execution %, Effective Call Rate.
  • Total Inventory Value, At-Risk Inventory %, Stock-Out Risk Items.
  • Top 1 Principal %, Compound Dependency Count, Qualified Backlog Count & Value.

Early warning KPIs

  • Watch category, Inactivity Signal, Due Within 7 Days, Credit Limit Signal.
  • Missing Target Setup, Bottom Visit Execution, GPS Valid Rate.
  • Slow Moving Count & Value, Never Sold after new intake.
  • Purchasing Inactivity Flag, Pending Posting Value.

Investigation KPIs

  • Named lists: Top Overdue Customers, Top 10 Outstanding Customers, Top 10 Dead / Slow Moving, Principal Exposure Comparison.
  • Action queues: Collection Action Queue and Inventory Action Counts By Type.
  • Salesman books: Top Overdue Salesmen, Dormant Portfolio Count.

Recommended reading order for a morning scan

  1. Customer health and overdue quality (not only Total Piutang).
  2. Collection action queue and Recovery vs Billing %.
  3. Salesman below-target and visit execution.
  4. Inventory at-risk share and stock-out list.
  5. Supplier concentration and qualified posting backlog.
