Owner Playbook · BTR Portal

Where to look. What it means. What to do next.

This is the owner’s guide to BTR Portal. Start from a business concern — not from a dashboard list. Each path tells you where to go, what to look at, how to read it, where evidence lives, and what decision is usually made.

1 Introduction

What this playbook is. A practical map from a business concern to a decision. It packages the questions BTR Portal can already answer. It does not add new KPIs, new screens, or new business theory.

How to use it. Bring one concern. Find the matching Executive Question. Open the Management Playbook underneath. Follow Open → Look At → If You See This → Evidence → Then Do This. Stop when you can name the action.

How the portal should be used. The portal is for judgment. Open it to see which area is weakest, then drill until names appear — customers, salespeople, SKUs, Principals. Do not start from a menu of dashboards and hope the right number appears.

How evidence supports decisions. A KPI tells you the size of the problem. Evidence names who or what owns it: attention lists, rankings, performance profiles, and portal reports (Sales, Piutang, Customer, Inventory, Purchasing). Decide only after the names are on the table.

Start from EQ-001. Do not start from a dashboard list.

Is the business healthy?

Why This Matters

The owner needs one morning judgment before opening any other screen. Customer quality, sales delivery, cash conversion, and warehouse capital can move in different directions on the same day.

EQ-002

Where is my biggest risk?

Why This Matters

Attention is scarce. Overdue customers, stuck stock, weak sales books, and Principal dependence can all look urgent. See which problem is largest before assigning work.

Related Management Questions

MQ-001 Which customers are becoming risky? MQ-002 Which inventory is unhealthy? MQ-003 Which salespeople carry overdue or dormant books? MQ-004 Which principals are a buying or stock risk?

Start Here

Management Attention Center, then the largest of the four playbooks

Compare Customers At Risk, At-Risk Inventory %, High Overdue Exposure, and Inventory Cross-Risk / Principal Dependency. Investigate the largest first.

Typical Decisions

  • Put collection, purchasing, or sales time on the largest problem — not on four problems at once.
  • Use Alert Center as a shortcut when exceptions are already flashing.
EQ-003

What needs immediate attention?

Why This Matters

Monthly totals do not tell the owner what must happen before the day ends. Today’s work is contacts, buying, delaying, or clearing.

Related Management Questions

MQ-005 Who should be contacted today? MQ-006 What should we buy, delay, transfer, or clear today?

Start Here

Collection Optimization Dashboard and / or Inventory Optimization Dashboard

Menu Customers → Collection Optimization · Menu Inventory → Inventory Optimization

Typical Decisions

  • Run today’s contact list by type — collect, remind, review credit, recover sales, or escalate.
  • Buy holes, delay piles, transfer, or clear — as separate actions, not one mixed shopping list.
EQ-004

Is cash flow healthy?

Why This Matters

Invoiced omzet is not cash. If billing outruns collection, the next purchase cycle is funded with delayed money.

Related Management Questions

MQ-007 Which receivables require attention? MQ-008 Are we collecting as fast as we are billing?

Start Here

Collection Dashboard, then Cash Flow Forecast Dashboard

Menu Finance → Collection · then Cash Flow Forecast. Start with Recovery vs Billing %.

Typical Decisions

  • Increase collection effort on named overdue accounts.
  • Slow new credit, or accept this month’s cash finish and plan from Expected / Worst Cash.
EQ-005

Is growth sustainable?

Why This Matters

A month can hit target on a few names while the rest of the book goes quiet. The owner needs to know whether growth will still be there after this month.

Related Management Questions

MQ-009 Which salespeople are underperforming? MQ-010 Which customers are declining?

Start Here

Salesman Performance, then Customer Portfolio Dashboard

Menu Sales Force → Salesmen · Menu Customers → Customer Portfolio. Start with Below Target Count and Declining Count.

Typical Decisions

  • Reallocate sales time, set missing targets, or coach named people.
  • Recover declining customers — or stop covering accounts that will not return.
EQ-006

Is working capital healthy?

Why This Matters

Cash sitting in dead stock and cash missing from empty selling SKUs are the same owner problem: money that cannot fund the next cycle in the right place.

Related Management Questions

MQ-011 Is warehouse capital trapped or about to run out?

Start Here

Slow Moving & Dead Stock Dashboard and Inventory Forecast Dashboard

Menu Inventory → Inventory Risk, then Inventory Forecast. Look at Dead Stock Value and Stock-Out Risk Items together.

Typical Decisions

  • Accelerate liquidation on idle stock, and buy only the holes.
  • Do not fund a full restock while dead stock is still the larger pile.
EQ-007

Are we overly dependent on specific customers, suppliers, or salespeople?

Why This Matters

A book, a sales force, or a warehouse can look healthy in total and still be one relationship away from a shock.

Related Management Questions

MQ-012 Which customers dominate omzet or piutang? MQ-013 Which suppliers are becoming dominant? MQ-014 Are we too dependent on a few salespeople?

Start Here

Customer Analytics, Purchasing Management, and Salesman Performance

Compare Top Omzet / Piutang Customer %, Top Principal %, and Top Omzet Salesman %.

Typical Decisions

  • Protect key accounts, reduce exposure, or broaden the book.
  • Diversify supplier exposure — or treat the dependence as chosen strategy.
  • Protect key people and rebuild coverage across the team.
EQ-008

What is likely to become a problem next?

Why This Matters

Overdue, dormant, dead stock, and Plafond breaches appear after the damage. The owner wants the signals that are forming now.

Related Management Questions

MQ-015 Is credit policy being respected? MQ-016 Are purchased goods posted, or stuck in backlog?

Start Here

Customer Analytics, then Purchasing Management Dashboard

Menu Customers → Customers (Plafond Breach, Suspended + Sales) · Menu Purchasing → Purchasing (Pending Posting, Purchasing Pace).

Typical Decisions

  • Review customer credit, restrict supply, or stop billing suspended accounts — while the failure is still preventable.
  • Post existing purchases first; resume buying only if selling SKUs are at stock-out risk.

MQ-001

Which customers are becoming risky?

Open Customer Risk Forecast · Look at Customers At Risk

Why Owner Asks This

Collection time and credit attention should go to accounts that are weakening, not only to accounts that are already overdue.

Typical trigger: collection slowing, cash flow pressure, portfolio health weakening, or customer complaints about supply holds.

Open This

Customer Risk Forecast Dashboard

Menu Customers → Customer Risk Forecast

Also visible as Customers At Risk on Management Attention Center and Customer Portfolio Dashboard. Start on Customer Risk Forecast because categories and signal families live there.

Look At

Primary KPICustomers At Risk
Supporting KPIPortfolio Health Score; Healthy / Watch / Attention / High Risk (and High / Critical Customers); Strategic At Risk; Payment Delay; Credit Limit; Inactivity; Purchase Decline; Elevated Risk Receivable

If You See This

HealthyMost of the book sits in Healthy. Customers At Risk and Strategic At Risk are not the story of the day. Signal families are quiet.
WarningWatch and Attention are filling. Payment Delay, Inactivity, or Purchase Decline is rising while totals still look manageable. Strategic customers appear in the at-risk count.
CriticalHigh Risk / Critical Customers and Elevated Risk Receivable are material versus Total Piutang. Strategic At Risk is no longer a small exception.

Do not invent numeric cut-offs. Use the risk categories and signal families the page already shows.

Then Do This

  • Increase collection effort on the named accounts.
  • Review customer credit.
  • Recover sales on declining or inactive names.
  • Restrict supply where credit or overdue is already chronic.

MQ-015

Is credit policy being respected?

Open Customer Analytics · Look at Plafond Breach and Credit Limit

Why Owner Asks This

Plafond and hold rules only protect cash if they are observed. A credit-limit signal is still preventable. A breach, or a suspended account still being billed, is already a failure.

MQ-016

Are purchased goods posted, or stuck in backlog?

Open Purchasing Management · Look at Pending Posting Value and Posted %

Why Owner Asks This

Unposted purchases mean the company has already spent or committed without gaining sellable stock. Quiet purchasing late in the month can be a freeze or a stall.

Typical trigger: warehouse waiting for stock that was already bought, purchasing gone quiet, or selling SKUs at risk of running out.

Open This

Purchasing Management Dashboard

Menu Purchasing → Purchasing · also Management Attention Center (Pending Posting) and Inventory Forecast for Stock-Out Risk Items

Look At

Primary KPIPending Posting Value and Posted %
Supporting KPIQualified Backlog; Grand Total Purchase; Purchasing Pace; Stock-Out Risk Items on Inventory Forecast

If You See This

HealthyPosted % is the bulk of invoices. Pending Posting Value and Qualified Backlog are small. Purchasing Pace is not an inactivity story while sales still have cover.
WarningPending Posting Value is material. Qualified Backlog is aging. Purchasing Pace shows the month going quiet after mid-month.
CriticalUnposted value is large and Stock-Out Risk Items is rising — money is committed but not sellable, while holes appear. Or purchasing is quiet while holes are already on Inventory Forecast.

Then Do This

  • Post existing invoices first.
  • Declare a freeze if inactivity is intentional.
  • Resume buying only the holes if Stock-Out Risk Items requires it.
